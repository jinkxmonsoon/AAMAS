# Final Do-Not-Claim List

## Scope

This list freezes forbidden manuscript claims after Task 31. It is intended for PMs, writers, and reviewers of manuscript drafts. It does not authorize new experiments, scoring, variants, hypothesis changes, or claim expansion.

## Forbidden claims

- Do not claim that CCT improves automatic failure attribution.
- Do not claim that CCT outperforms baselines.
- Do not claim that CCT is robust under perturbations.
- Do not claim that CCT scoring is ready for calibration.
- Do not claim that H1, H2, H1-R, or H2-R are supported.
- Do not claim that H3/H4 are unblocked.
- Do not claim that H6 or H6-R is supported.
- Do not claim that the causal-flow redesign validates the original CCT hypothesis.
- Do not claim that the final rescue results are paper-ready performance superiority evidence.
- Do not claim production readiness or external-corpus generalization.
- Do not claim formal causal identification; "causal-flow" remains operational evidence-flow terminology.
- Do not claim that leakage PASS implies attribution performance.
- Do not claim that within-trace feature distinguishability implies scoring readiness.
- Do not claim that the omitted `flat_log_text_similarity_baseline` was evaluated.
- Do not claim that additional variants, tuned weights, calibration, ablation, LOSO, grid search, or statistical tests were run.

## Required replacement framing

- Use "diagnostic representation" instead of "automatic attribution improvement."
- Use "negative evidence under current features" instead of "CCT performance result."
- Use "protocol-first benchmark and audit foundation" instead of "production-ready method."
- Use "current featureization is insufficient" instead of "CCT is disproven in general."
- Use "Path B negative-evidence framing" instead of "performance-oriented method paper."

## Draft-review rule

Any draft paragraph that implies baseline superiority, robustness, calibration readiness, external validity, production readiness, or formal causal identification must be revised or removed unless a future explicitly authorized protocol produces new evidence.
